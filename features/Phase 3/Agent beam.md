While a local agent works in the vault, a beam of light in its colours rides round the window's edge, one per agent.

> [!use] Using it
> Turn on each agent to watch in Settings › AI: Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Cursor or Antigravity. That puts Muta's hooks into the agent's own settings, for every project (Codex runs them once you approve them in its `/hooks` screen; OpenCode loads its plugin when its background service restarts). Then run it on notes in the vault. While it's working, a beam of light in the agent's colours (Claude Code's terracotta, Codex's blues, OpenCode's greens, Antigravity's Google colours, Cursor's silvers) rides round the page's edge, inside the window's frame, fading out as soon as it finishes. While one waits on you to allow something (Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode ask; Cursor and Antigravity don't say), its beam keeps turning but breathes, dimming and brightening, until you answer. Settings › AI also turns the beams off. Experimental, like everything agents do in Muta.

> [!how] How it works
> Each agent tells Muta what it's doing through its own hooks, which `AgentWatch.setHooks()` in `main.js` puts in and takes out, leaving the user's own: Claude Code's in `~/.claude/settings.json` (a prompt, each tool call and its result, a permission request, the end of a turn, an API error, sitting idle, the end of a session), Codex's in `~/.codex/hooks.json` (a prompt, each tool call and its result, a permission request, the end or interruption of a turn, the end of a session), Cursor's in `~/.cursor/hooks.json`, Antigravity's in `~/.gemini/config/hooks.json` (each model call and tool, the end of a turn), and for OpenCode 2 a plugin in `~/.config/opencode/plugins/muta` (its turns starting and ending, its permissions asked and replied, and its tools). Each runs a script in `~/.muta` (`hookScript()`; the plugin does the same itself) that adds the event, a JSON line, to `~/.muta/events/<agent>-<date>.jsonl`, but only while Muta is running: while some vault's mark in `~/.muta/running` was touched in the last 5 minutes (each vault touches its own every few seconds; unloading lets it lapse in a minute, so a reload carries on). So nothing piles up while Muta isn't running, and files untouched for two days are deleted. Every vault reads the files (watching the folder, and every 4s anyway), each keeping its own place in them, so several vaults can watch at once, and a reload reads the sessions back where they are; after a while with no Muta running at all, those read back count as stopped, since their ends may have gone by unrecorded. `agentEvent()` turns each agent's events into one shape: a prompt or a tool call means it's working, the end of a turn (or an interruption, or a failure) that it isn't, a permission request that it's waiting on you until its next event (the tool's result, a reply, the turn ending), and a subagent's work counts as its agent's, and edits (Claude Code's Edit, MultiEdit and Write, Codex's patches, OpenCode's edit and write, Cursor's file edits, Antigravity's `edit_file` and `write_file`) say which files and what's new. With each event the script records the agent's own process (the first above it that isn't a shell; OpenCode's plugin, its service's), and a session whose process has gone ends at once: a crash, a kill, or a run that quit mid-turn without its end-of-turn hook (as Antigravity's headless runs do when a command is denied). A session quiet for 30 minutes counts as stopped, unless it's waiting on you and still running. The waiting beam (`.is-waiting`) keeps its place in the turn, so the beams stay spaced, and pulses its opacity every 1.4s; with Reduce motion it's dimmed instead. It's working in the vault when it runs from inside the vault or edited one of its notes in the last five minutes, including notes the vault links to from elsewhere (symlinks, as the test vault's `features/` is). With **Agents in linked projects** on (Settings › AI, off by default), an agent working anywhere in a project the vault links into counts too, though it edits no notes: the git repository holding each linked folder (`vaultRoots()`'s `projects`), but not one round the whole home folder. On load, the hooks of every agent turned on are brought up to date, and an earlier version's are cleared away. The beam follows [Beam](https://libraries.dev/beam) (`styles.css`): the agent's colours fixed round the edge (`data-agent` on each beam), shown through an arc that turns every 2.6s, as a 1.5px stroke round the page panel with a white head, a haze of its colours fading into the page over 74px, and a soft bloom at its head. All of it is masked rather than blurred (no CSS filters), so several beams at once stay at full frame rate. The window's outer edge catches its light, like the rim of macOS's glass (`.muta-agent-rim`), and so do the lower edges of the two pills of buttons beside the notch (`.muta-agent-keys`: the same light round the window's centre, in the top bar, masked to the lower half of the pills' outlines, fading in from their middle, which `keysOutline()` draws as an SVG as the window docks or changes size): a softer, wider band of the same colours round where the head is, and a fringe a few degrees ahead with its colours shifted, splitting the light as glass does. With several agents at work at once, each has its own beam (and rim), spread evenly round the edge and shortened to its share (`--muta-beam-offset`, `--muta-beam-span`), all turning together (each starts at the same point of the 2.6s turn); they slide apart or together over 0.7s as agents start and finish, up to four. Each fades in over 0.6s and out over 0.5s; with Reduce motion it holds still and comes and goes at once.
