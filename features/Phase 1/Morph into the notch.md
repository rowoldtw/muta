At the top middle of a MacBook with a notch, the window hangs from the notch, morphs into it, and grows back out when you hover it.

> [!use] Using it
> Throw or drag the window to the top middle of a screen with a camera notch. It hangs from the top of the screen shaped like the notch, square along the screen's edge and curving out into it with concave shoulders, styled like Siri's: a heavy blur, black where it meets the notch and easing into a smoky grey that clears towards the bottom, its top bar beside the notch (back and forward on the left, edit, Muta's menu and More options on the right, each group joined in one hairline border like the page's and centred beside the notch, always shown and still) and its page its own panel below. Once the cursor leaves it, it turns black and shrinks into the notch; a menu opened from it (More options, Muta's menu, the bottom bar's list) holds it out until the menu closes, and picking from the bottom bar's list holds it out two seconds more, so a pick low in the list (below the window) shows the page picked. It's dark in either theme, and the bottom bar's buttons sit on a soft dark fill with the hairline of the buttons beside the notch, so their light text reads over a white window below. Move the cursor onto the notch, or hover a link in Obsidian, and it grows back out (a hovered link holds it out two seconds; stepping through pages from inside the window doesn't); it goes back in a moment after the cursor leaves. The top bar opens only once the cursor has been down into the window, not on its way down from the notch. Drag its top bar, or swipe on it, to take it out. On a screen without a notch the top middle is an ordinary resting spot.

> [!how] How it works
> `glass.node` finds each screen's notch (`notches()`: the gap between `auxiliaryTopLeftArea` and `auxiliaryTopRightArea`, `safeAreaInsets.top` tall) and lets the window into the menu bar (`allowAboveMenuBar()`: Electron's `enableLargerThanScreen`, which skips AppKit's keep-it-below-the-menu-bar rule); docked, it sits over the menu bar (`status` level). The window never moves or resizes, since resizing a web page every frame stutters. Docked, the window starts `LIFT` (28px) above the screen, so macOS's own rounded window corners are out of sight, and the page sits that far down and `SHOULDER` (14px) in from each side. Its Liquid Glass gives way to a heavier macOS blur (`NSVisualEffectView`, HUD material, its backdrop's Gaussian blur turned up to 40px through Core Animation's private filters: `blurRadius`), with the material's own grey tint layers and colour filters taken away (`pure`), so the tint in CSS is the only shading, which has no glare, cross-fading so it doesn't flash; `glass.node` masks it to the notch's shape with a Core Animation shape layer (`shape()`) and drops the window's shadow, whose edge would outline it. A thin glare runs just inside the edge, like Liquid Glass's rim: a gradient seen through a stroke of the same path, brightest along the bottom and gone by the notch, morphing and fading with the blur. Behind the page, `html::before` draws the same shape (a `clip-path` path from `hangingPath()`) with Siri's tint: near-black at the notch, easing to a light smoky tint at the bottom, over a lighter page panel. It takes the docked look as it flies up, so arriving is one motion. Going in, the shape turns black and morphs into the notch's own (the two paths have the same segments, so CSS interpolates them; `is-notched`); the blur's mask morphs in step, with the same timing (`shape()`), so the window is blurred from the moment it starts growing out; once it's in, the blur goes. In the notch the window is see-through and lets clicks through, so `pip-main.js` checks the cursor every 50ms, and its own position: macOS can move a window that's partly above the screen back onto it as windows and Spaces change, so a docked window that's been moved is put straight back (`holdAtNotch()`). Hovering a link keeps it out for 2s. Obsidian sets the page's theme classes when it likes (as the window opens, on a theme change), so `main.js` watches the page's classes and puts `theme-dark` back at once while it's docked (`keepTheme()`, `windowTheme()`), and Obsidian's own theme on leaving, read from Obsidian's window so a change made while docked is followed; should a light theme slip in, `styles.css` keeps Muta's own colours dark. With Reduce motion it switches without animating.
